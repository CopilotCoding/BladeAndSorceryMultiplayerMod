## Native death-flow isolation

Grip/time diagnostics exposed a real lifecycle conflict: Level.OnCreatureKilled started its single-player defeat coroutine during multiplayer respawn. That coroutine opened the defeat menu, blocked the player menu, disabled locomotion and ran death slow motion at scale 0.1 even after a replacement body had spawned. This also contaminated fast physical-combat fixtures.

SessionDeathFlowPatch now suppresses only that level handler for the local player during a connected session. Native Creature.Kill, item releases, ragdoll behavior and kill events still run. The match freezes dead-player locomotion until the native spawner restores a body. If the player disconnects while still dead and no respawn is in flight, the saved level handler is invoked after unpatching to restore native single-player defeat behavior without replaying kill events.

All seven initial dedicated death-flow checks passed on mod MVID cd11c68753b34f61b47d164606799656. Current regression fixtures also require usable menus and normal time after replacement-body respawn. Local events skip replay in ApplyAction, so each owner must still schedule respawn through its local death callback; host scoring uses that callback for local deaths and routed events for remote deaths. NPC kills cannot be credited to their simulation owner's player, and outgoing score state excludes departed peers. Lifecycle checks require exactly one replicated death-score increment.

## Mock tracking origin

New mock bodies reset head/hand targets, held input, scripted trajectories and velocities. Explicit fixture poses reset native IK only while hands and body grips are free; snapping a constrained body independently of its weapon would create a false combat impulse. The first received creature snapshot snaps the replica once, after any pending native slices have completed. Subsequent motion uses interpolation and physical drives. Native character-joint anchors are retargeted from the tracked owner poses to avoid an NPC rest skeleton resisting the owner's IK geometry; physical ragdolls restore their original anchors.

Registered network-player body handles retain native grip joints and colliders. Scoped patches substitute remote controller transforms where native HandleRagdoll assumes a local PlayerHand, and prevent NPC posture, arm pin-force and state changes from overriding VR tracking. A grappled limb yields to the grip rather than receiving a second pose drive. Its held weapon uses finite mass coupling during the grapple. Received body grips preserve the target owner's existing kinematic mode. The body-grab fixture uses reachable fighting poses and checks native reach, replicated grip/release, root displacement, body velocities, held swords and locomotion on both peers. Hands without a native handle are recorded as unsupported fixture targets rather than reported as passing grabs.

The remote held-item collision filter now uses the same opposite-hand/arm exclusions as native Item.RefreshCollision. The earlier mapping reversed left and right, potentially enabling collisions between a held sword and its own constrained hand. Grip-alignment and body-grab tests compare the actual Unity collider-pair ignore flags against the native owner on both peers. Grapple cases retain one equipped sword throughout each sequence and position fighters only before equipping, avoiding unrelated repeated native teleports inside a held-constraint check.

The automated driver accepts world-space pose commands but stores them relative to Player.local.transform, as a real XR origin does. Native locomotion, every native teleport path and respawn therefore carry the scripted head/hands together. The previous world-space storage only rebased explicitly handled teleports; a native spawner teleport could leave stale head coordinates and drive the mock body far from its feet. That contaminated co-op melee-range checks after long combined runs. This component remains exclusive to the isolated test runtime. Co-op approach fixtures also project onto the native navigation mesh instead of carrying an airborne height into the next move.

## Source-native player cut requests and consistency checks

The controller change alone did not solve unreliable neck cuts. Source contacts could pass native cutting requirements while the independently simulated receiving blade missed the required speed. Player-held/thrown weapons now capture native player cut candidates at Damager.TryPerformSlice, after the native collision has passed its material, blade-angle, speed and penetration prerequisites. NPC/world combat retains its existing ownership behavior. The hook returns false while the request is pending rather than falsely reporting a local slice.

The first request implementation re-ran CheckSlice against the victim's current pose. That still reproduced a real failure: in a longsword run on mod 54cf0d6cfbb2497789791838c789b57e, only one of three live swings cut the neck. A native source cut at 13.61 m/s was rejected by the receiving slice plane. The original contact was relative to the head while the requested cut was the neck; independent head/neck motion and presentation delay changed that relationship. Those reports are preserved in artifacts/cut-request-v1-investigation. The earlier retry-based PASS label overstated consistency and must not be used as evidence of a reliable fix.

Version 2 requests carry each candidate's original native plane-distance and blade-up/forward dot products. The victim validates those measurements against the native part width, weapon tier and angle limits, along with material/depth rules, ownership/epoch, content settings, invincibility and match protection. It applies RagdollPart.TrySlice and native fatal-part Kill normally; existing owner callbacks publish slice/death consequences. The receiving weapon's native slice cooldown is updated. No blade/part threshold or collider is widened. These are trusted client contact reports, not an anti-cheat system. Reliable gameplay deduplication and native already-sliced/dead guards remain active.

An invincibility snapshot bit (512) prevents protected remote contacts from requesting cuts; the owner checks protection again on arrival. The protocol decoder now accepts defined flags through 1023 and still rejects unknown bits. Round-trip and malformed-flag protocol checks cover this addition; exact mod/core build negotiation prevents mixing old decoders with this build. The snapshot layout and maximum packet size are unchanged.

The first source-only request build inadvertently disabled the victim's own legitimate native cuts. ## PvP penetration and withdrawal update (0.1.3)

The subsequent real Index session reported Slaughter Sword failures in the abdomen/chest and a dagger that stopped stabbing after initially working. The practice-side saved state identifies DaggerPierce as a weapon used in this session, rather than the DaggerCommon used by the earlier automated checks. No active normal-game combat capture was saved, so the exact failed source contact cannot be reconstructed from those logs. The normal game and practice client had both exited before this investigation; the available shutdown exceptions were in the native remote inspector and OpenVR shutdown, not a captured stabbing stack trace.

Code review found concrete failure paths: the replicated-penetration setup could reuse an active collision slot that already held a different penetration, replacing its damager metadata and creating another joint without clearing the previous one. Its exit matching also depended on current collider indices. Finally, the same owner filter used to decide new penetrations blocked local native teardown on a non-owner even when the joint or target had become invalid. These paths can leave ignored collisions and stale penetration bookkeeping. They are plausible causes of the dagger report; that causal link is not established by a source capture.

PvP player weapons now use the weapon owner's native contact/pressure penetration and withdrawal decisions. This removes the requirement for the delayed remote grip to independently qualify the stab or pull its copy out before the actual wielder can finish withdrawal. NPC and other penetration retain their prior authority paths. Source-authoritative penetration events use Entity=weapon, Other=player; other constraints retain the original mapping. Native local joint anchors, axes, depth, blade point and held-weapon pose behavior are preserved.

Each native entry receives a session-unique penetration token, carried with both entry and exit. A CollisionInstance binding retains original actor/target/part/collider metadata so an exit does not become a different stab after slicing, collider reordering or ownership changes. Mirror records retain the exact native joint and check its object identity before teardown, preventing a delayed exit from clearing a replacement joint in a reused slot. Slot reuse is limited to non-penetrating ordinary contacts; native by-group/by-collider matching is respected. Native pass-through exit callbacks keep their wentThrough flag. Incoming constraints are released before remote targets are removed. Native invalid-joint/disabled-target cleanup is allowed even on a mirror.

Moving native stab simulation also moves initial and deep-damage callbacks. Positive native PvP weapon damage is therefore carried through reliable weapon-hit requests, including normal slash/blunt weapon impacts to keep one damage producer per player weapon. The victim applies the native damage amount through Creature.Damage after protection/material/epoch checks and presentation delay. Its normal owner callbacks publish health, death, score and respawn. Mirrored weapon simulation does not apply duplicate positive damage. Direct fatal native slices retain the existing native kill path. Native zero-damage callbacks and non-weapon/NPC damage stay on their previous paths. These trusted contact reports are not an anti-cheat system; armor/skill edge cases and broader PvE still need runtime validation.

Source-native cut requests now also preserve the source damager's native no-penetration cooldown and clear that hit's pending penetration mode, preventing a queued cut from simultaneously creating a stabbing joint. Optional combat captures add collision-slot, live-joint, orphaned-penetration and part-blocking counters to diagnose any remaining loss of stabbing. Session/scene cleanup clears the new bindings and pending damage/cut queues.

This update is build-checked only. The user's instruction to skip automated game tests remains in force; no new game instances or functional test suites were started for this change. The versioned archive records skipped verification explicitly. A new in-game check is still needed to establish whether the reported Slaughter Sword and dagger failures are resolved.
